**Date:** 2026-08-06
**Type:** Read-only audit. No application code, schema, data or configuration was modified.
**Scope:** The **CRM** application only. Transaction Management (Transaction Desk) is excluded.

## AUDIT METHOD AND ITS LIMITS

Findings below are drawn from source code, the Prisma schema, migrations, the running application,
and the existing automated test suites (run, not modified). Where a workflow could not be traced end
to end, it is labelled **Not Verified** rather than assumed working.

**Two limits on this audit, stated up front:**

1. **The working tree contains substantial uncommitted work.** The MFA system, the notification
   platform (Centre, per-channel preferences, dispatcher), the Redis/queue layer and chat mentions
   are present in the tree but **not committed to git**. This audit describes the tree as it stands.
   Anything deployed from the last commit would not include them.
2. **No production environment was inspected.** All verification was against the development and
   test databases on one machine.

### How CRM scope was determined
Not by page names. The authoritative classification is `server/src/common/domain.ts`
(`SCREEN_DOMAIN`) and its client twin `client/src/desk/area.ts` (`SCREEN_AREA`):

| Domain | Screens |
|---|---|
| **`crm`** | `lead`, `campaigns`, `meta`, `reviews` |
| **`common`** (CRM has its own view) | `inbox`, `dashboard`, `calendar`, `audit`, `triggers`, `settings`, `users` |
| **`desk`** — excluded from this audit | `transactions`, `invoice`, `reports`, `analytics`, `recycle-bin`, `mls`, `favorites`, `inventory` |

**This settles two questions immediately:** Recycle Bin is `desk`, so the CRM has none (§15); and
Analytics, MLS, Favorites and Inventory are Transaction Desk screens, not CRM.

# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

## What the CRM is
A customer-relationship system for a Canadian real-estate brokerage (Get Home Realty), operating
beside a separate Transaction Desk in the same application shell. It manages the **lead lifecycle**:
intake (manual, Meta Lead Ads, bulk import), ownership and assignment, follow-up activity, email
marketing, a per-user mailbox, a calendar, and an audit trail.

## Business processes supported
Lead capture and qualification · lead ownership and assignment · activity logging (notes, tasks,
calls, showings, emails) · appointment scheduling and reminders · email marketing with CASL
compliance · per-agent mailbox · brokerage-level CRM configuration · audit history.

## Who uses it
Roles defined in `server/src/auth/permission.service.ts`: **Super Admin** (`admin`), **Admin /
Manager** (`manager`), **Agent** (`agent`), **CRM/Marketing** (`crm`), plus `accounting` and
`documentation` (Desk-oriented, minimal CRM reach).

## Module count
**10 CRM modules** with real implementations, plus one nav entry that is a placeholder.

## Overall maturity
High for the core lead lifecycle and the supporting platform. The codebase is unusually
well-documented — most non-obvious decisions carry a written rationale — and test coverage is
substantial and, in the areas examined, genuinely assertive rather than decorative.

## Overall reliability
Good. **1330/1330 server tests pass** across 90 suites; **374 end-to-end tests** exist across 25
files. Background work is gated so a second process cannot duplicate it, external failures are
recorded rather than swallowed, and the notification platform degrades predictably.

## Overall production readiness

### **PRODUCTION READY WITH CONDITIONS**

**Why "with conditions" rather than "ready":** there are no known data-loss defects, no known
authorization holes, and no broken core workflow. But four operational conditions must be met before
deployment, none of which is a code fix:

1. Four database migrations in the tree are **not applied to production**.
2. `APP_KEY` must be set, or authenticator-based two-factor enrolment is refused by design.
3. Credentials exposed earlier in development **still require rotation**.
4. The work described here is **uncommitted**; it must be committed and deployed as a unit.

**Why not lower:** the two authentication vulnerabilities found during this project (session
fixation; ILIKE wildcard login) are fixed and sensitivity-checked. Lead visibility is enforced
server-side with a single shared rule. Meta lead intake is idempotent and budget-limited.

# 4. CRM ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW

## Frontend
- **Framework:** React 18 + TypeScript, built with Vite. No Redux or React Query — hooks and context only.
- **Routing:** `react-router-dom`. Routes are generated from a single `SCREENS` table in
  `client/src/App.tsx`, per area (`/crm/...`, `/desk/...`), each wrapped in `RequireScreen`.
- **Component architecture:** page components under `client/src/desk/`, lazily loaded.
- **API communication:** one axios instance (`client/src/lib/axios.ts`) with `withCredentials`,
  `withXSRFToken`, and a 419 interceptor that refetches CSRF and replays once.
- **Authentication handling:** `AuthContext` restores the session via `GET /api/user`; login may now
  return a two-factor challenge instead of a user.
- **State:** local component state plus context. No global store.

## Backend
- **Framework:** NestJS 10 on Express.
- **Modules:** feature modules per domain (`leads`, `meta`, `campaigns`, `calendar`, `inbox`,
  `crm-settings`, `dashboard`, `audit`, `notifications`, `auth`, `core`).
- **Guards:** `AuthGuard` (session), `AdminGuard` (top tier), `ScreenGuard` (screen × level ×
  area), `CsrfGuard` (global), `IdentityThrottlerGuard` (global rate limiting).
- **Validation:** `class-validator` DTOs with a Laravel-shaped 422 error format.
- **Background jobs:** in-process timers, gated by `RUN_SCHEDULERS`, each registered with a
  worker-health registry surfaced at `/api/health/workers`.

## Database
- **PostgreSQL 17**, accessed through **Prisma 6**.
- **Multi-tenancy:** a Prisma client extension (`core/tenant-extension.ts`) adds `company_id` to
  every query against any model carrying that column. Models are classified ROOT / DERIVED / GLOBAL
  and `core/tenancy.spec.ts` **fails the build** if a new table is unclassified.
- **Key CRM models:** `leads`, `lead_notes`, `lead_tasks`, `lead_calls`, `lead_showings`,
  `lead_emails`, `lead_messages`, `lead_tags`, `campaigns`, `campaign_recipients`,
  `campaign_templates`, `campaign_links`, `campaign_clicks`, `email_suppressions`,
  `meta_connections`, `meta_pages`, `meta_lead_forms`, `meta_sync_history`, `meta_webhook_events`,
  `calendar_events`, `calendar_event_reminders`, `mail_accounts`, `inbound_emails`,
  `crm_settings`, `crm_email_settings`, `crm_trigger_settings`, `crm_referral_codes`,
  `crm_broadcasts`, `audit_logs`, `notifications`, `notification_preferences`.

## B. How The Module Works

**User's view:** an agent opens **Lead**, sees only their own leads, searches or filters, opens one,
logs a call/note/task, schedules a showing, or emails the lead. An administrator sees the
brokerage's intake as well as their own.

**Technical flow:** `LeadsPage` → axios → `LeadsController` → `AuthGuard` (session → user →
tenant) → `ScreenGuard` (screen × level × area) → `LeadsService` → `leadScopeWhere(user)` merged
into the Prisma `where` → tenant extension adds `company_id` → Postgres.

## C. Feature Workflow — Create Lead

1. Agent opens **Lead** and selects Add.
2. Form validates required fields client-side.
3. `POST /api/leads` with the payload.
4. `CsrfGuard` verifies `X-XSRF-TOKEN` against the session.
5. `AuthGuard` resolves the session to a user and sets the tenant.
6. `ScreenGuard` requires `lead: edit`.
7. DTO validation returns a 422 in Laravel shape on failure.
8. Service writes the lead, stamping `owner_user_id` and `company_id`.
9. `LeadAuditService` records the creation in `audit_logs`.
10. Response returns the created lead; the list refetches.

## 🔴 Structural risk worth naming
**`ScreenGuard` is not registered globally.** A controller that carries `@Screen(...)` but omits
`ScreenGuard` from `@UseGuards` enforces **nothing** — the decorators become comments. This exact
mistake was made and caught during recent development on the MFA admin controller.

**Severity: Medium (latent).** No CRM controller is currently affected — `leads.controller.ts`
carries both guards, as do campaigns, crm-settings, dashboard and roles. But the pattern is a
standing trap for the next controller.

# 41. HOW THE CRM CURRENTLY WORKS
### A guide for a new developer, tester, BA, manager or support employee

**Signing in.** You reach a login page and enter a username (or email) and password. Matching is
case-insensitive. Too many wrong attempts locks that account for a while. If you have set up
two-factor authentication, you are asked for a six-digit code from your authenticator app, or one
sent by email or text; a recovery code also works, once. You can tick "do not ask again on this
device" to skip the code for 30 days on that browser.

**Finding your way.** The application has two halves — **CRM** and **Transaction Management** — and
a switcher between them. The menu only shows what your role may open.

**The Dashboard** is the first CRM screen. It shows your leads and outstanding tasks. Agents see
their own; administrators additionally see the brokerage's intake. The tiles and the Leads screen use
the same rule, so they always agree.

**Leads** is the heart of the CRM. A lead can arrive three ways: typed in by hand, imported in bulk
from a spreadsheet, or pulled automatically from Facebook Lead Ads. Each lead has an owner and an
assignee. Opening one shows everything about it: notes, follow-up tasks, calls (which can be dialled
from the browser and recorded), showings, emails and text messages. You can search, filter, sort and
act on several at once. Deleting a lead hides it and it can be restored.

**Meta** connects the brokerage's Facebook account. Once connected, the CRM checks every fifteen
minutes for new leads from your ad forms and creates them automatically, skipping anyone already in
the system. Access tokens are encrypted. There is a shared budget so one person pressing "Sync now"
repeatedly cannot exhaust the allowance everyone else depends on.

**Calendar** holds appointments and viewings, in day, week or month view, including repeating events.
You can change or cancel a single occurrence or the whole series. Reminders reach you in the app, by
email, and as a push notification. It can also sync both ways with Google Calendar.

**Inbox** is your own mailbox inside the CRM. You connect an email account once; the CRM then checks
it in the background and shows new mail, matching messages to leads where it can. You can search,
read and reply. Your CRM mailbox is separate from any Transaction Desk mailbox.

**Campaigns** sends marketing email. You choose an audience from your leads, pick or write a
template, and schedule it. The CRM tracks who opened and who clicked, retries soft failures, stops
sending to addresses that hard-bounce, and honours unsubscribes as Canadian anti-spam law requires.

**Triggers** controls which automatic CRM emails *you* send. The setting is yours alone.

**Notifications** has two screens. **Notifications** is the centre: everything you have been told,
with history, filters, search, and mark-as-read. **Notification Preferences** is a grid where you
choose how each kind of notification reaches you — in the app, by email, by push — independently.
Turning off the phone buzz leaves the email arriving.

**CRM Settings** holds brokerage configuration: the CRM email account, referral codes, and a
broadcast to all staff.

**Audit Trail** records who changed what and when. The CRM trail and the Transaction Desk trail are
kept apart, so you only see entries from the side you are on.

**How it fits together.** A Facebook lead arrives → becomes a CRM lead owned by the brokerage →
appears on your Leads screen and Dashboard → you log a call and set a follow-up → the follow-up
becomes an appointment → the calendar reminds you in the app, by email and on your phone → you email
the lead from the Inbox → later they are included in a campaign audience → every one of those
changes leaves an entry in the Audit Trail.

**Two things to know as a newcomer.**
"Client Reviews" appears in the menu but is not built — it shows a "Coming soon" placeholder.
And the CRM does not currently notify you when a new lead is assigned to you; you find out by
looking. Both are recorded in this report.
